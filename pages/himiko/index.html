Emma Himiko Leonhart

the hidden name

Emma H Leonhart is the name on my documents. The H is Himiko — a name I built in kanji, read three ways at once.

卑弥呼 Himiko

Himiko was the shaman-queen of Yamatai, a third-century ruler remembered in the Chinese chronicles — one of the first named rulers of Japan, and about as much legend as history, the way King Arthur is. The H in Emma H Leonhart stands for her. Legally I'm Emma H Leonhart; Himiko is the part I don't spell out.

閻魔日巫女獅心 the reading

Written in kanji, my name is 閻魔日巫女獅心 — three names in one, with a couple of readings I chose myself (one with a historical basis, one just for the sound). It splits into Emma, Himiko, Leonhart:

閻魔

Enma · Emma

The judge of the dead in Buddhist tradition — and, read aloud, a near-pun on Emma.

日

Hi · sun

Sun, day, and Japan itself. The first half of Himiko, and the light in it.

巫女

Miko · shrine maiden

A Shinto shrine maiden — the one who stands between the everyday and the sacred.

獅心

Shishin · Leonhart

Lion's heart, rendered whole — Leonhart, translated rather than transliterated.

One name, two worlds

It's built to work in both. In English, Emma Himiko Leonhart reads as an ordinary — if slightly unusual — name. In Japanese, the kanji open into something older: a judge, a shrine maiden, a lion's heart. That's roughly the split I live in anyway — English and Japanese, Anglican and Shinto — so the name just says it in one line.

閻魔王 a footnote

There's a loose nod to Oda Nobunaga in the 閻魔. He styled himself 第六天魔王 — Demon King of the Sixth Heaven. Mine rhymes with that without going quite so far; if I'm being formal about it, the long way to write the H is 閻魔王, Enma-ō.
