Emma Himiko Leonhart

the hidden name

Emma H Leonhart is the name I present to the world — the armor. Himiko is the name beneath it: the soul, the cipher, the flag planted in the ground.

卑弥呼 Himiko

Himiko was the legendary shaman-queen of Yamatai, a third-century polity remembered in the Chinese chronicles — a ruler who mediated between the human and the divine, semi-mythical and mysterious the way King Arthur is. Divine authority, spiritual leadership, an ancient and regal aura.

The H in Emma H Leonhart is Himiko. It is the middle initial you can read past — or read into. Legally I am Emma H Leonhart; Himiko is the hidden name. Not hidden out of shame — kept as a sacred, personal truth, the part of me that does not need to be on display to be real.

閻魔日巫女獅心 the cipher

A name written to reward those who take the time to unpack it — with two kun'yomi readings I invented myself, one historically plausible and one kira-kira. Four glyphs, four claims:

閻魔

Enma · judgment

The king of hell in Buddhist tradition — the judge. Authority, and a divine wrath.

日

Hi · the sun

Sun, day, and Japan itself — brightness, clarity, a cultural anchor. The light in Himiko.

巫女

Miko · the mediator

The shrine maiden — the bridge between the sacred and the mundane, Shinto ritual and purity.

獅心

Shishin · lion's heart

Courage, ferocity, nobility. Leonhart, rendered whole: the lion's heart, unshakeable.

A unification

The name is deliberately built to work in two worlds. To a Western ear Emma Himiko Leonhart reads as a high-society name with a touch of the exotic; to a Japanese reader the kanji open into something spiritual, powerful, and constructed. It can sound like an anime character or like a WASP, depending on who is listening — and that duality is the point.

It ties together the things I hold in parallel: Anglo-Saxon power and Japanese myth, Anglicanism and Shinto, old-money pragmatism and divine authority. I am both the judge (閻魔) and the mediator (巫女), the divine (Himiko) and the pragmatic. It is not conforming to any group; it is a name that makes its own category.

第六天魔王 → 閻魔王

There is a loose Oda Nobunaga reference in it. He styled himself 第六天魔王 — Demon King of the Sixth Heaven. I call myself 閻魔 — or, if I am being more creative, 閻魔王, King Enma.

I am not just one thing. I am a synthesis of histories, spiritualities, and ambitions — the judge and the mediator, the divine and the pragmatic.
